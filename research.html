Working papers

When Privacy Protects but Excludes: The Costs and Benefits of Privacy Regulation in Credit Markets

with Sumit Agarwal, Pulak Ghosh, Peiyi Jin, Shohini Kundu, Nishant Vats, and Xinbo Wang

Revise and resubmit, Review of Financial Studies

SSRN VoxEU column

Abstract

This paper studies the consequences of privacy regulation by exploiting Google's 2019 data-access restriction for a major Indian FinTech lender. Stronger privacy protections increase loan applications but induce tighter screening and an overall contraction in credit supply, disproportionately excluding economically and socially marginalized applicants. Linking to economy-wide credit-bureau records, we show that privacy-induced rejection reduces the probability of obtaining any formal credit by 13.7 percentage points even four years later. A structural model shows that the regulation increases consumer surplus by 0.23–0.60% while reducing lender profits by 20–23%.

Promoting Gender Equality or Crowding Out? Evaluating the Impact of China's 2019 Anti-Discrimination Notice on Women's Employment

Solo-authored · First-year paper

Abstract

This project evaluates the effects of China's 2019 Anti-Discrimination Notice, which restricts employers from asking candidates about their marital and fertility status. Preliminary results suggest that the policy increased young women's employment rate by approximately 2–4 percentage points.

Common Ownership Directors

with Ofer Eldar and Yaron Nili

Abstract

Using data on overlapping directors and institutional shareholdings from 2000 to 2019, this project shows that common ownership among firms in the same industry is associated with a greater likelihood of sharing directors. The relationship is particularly strong for institutions with lower portfolio turnover and longer investment horizons. The project highlights a potential mechanism through which common ownership affects managerial incentives.

Sales Losses and Incumbent Adjustment Following Dollar-Store Entry

Solo-authored

Summary

This project studies how incumbent convenience stores respond when a dollar store opens nearby. Using point-of-sale register data covering 3.4 billion transactions, preliminary results show that a dollar-store opening within two miles reduces a neighboring convenience store's non-licensed merchandise sales by about eight percent, while tobacco, alcohol, and fuel are unaffected. Incumbents show no detectable change in prices, promotions, package sizes, or assortment breadth. A structural model asks what this non-response costs the incumbent.

Equity Constraints in Financing: Do They Deter Moral Hazard or Reduce Investment?

with Pulak Ghosh, Nishant Vats, and Constantine Yannelis

Summary

This project studies the policy trade-off created by equity requirements. Such requirements may reduce moral hazard by increasing firms' exposure to project losses, but they may also limit investment by constraining financing for wealth-constrained entrepreneurs. The analysis examines the relative importance of these forces for the design of financial regulation.
